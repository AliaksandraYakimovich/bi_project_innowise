Data Storytelling & Business Insights Report
Project: Customer Lifecycle & Operations Analytics Suite

Executive Summary
In this report, I translate the insights from my Power BI solution into clear business actions. The solution connects big-picture metrics (revenue trends and product sales) with daily operational tools for managers. By analyzing customer data from 2020 to 2024, I found key trends in customer behavior, revenue risks, and bottlenecks that need our attention.

1. Key Pattern Identification & Analysis
Pattern 1: The "At-Risk" Revenue Paradox (Strategic Insight)
What I found: Looking at the RFM segmentation, the "At Risk" group is substantial. It holds 1,973 customers (about 43% of the total base of 4,602 users) and brings in the largest share of historical revenue — over $612.18 million (with an overall historical portfolio value reaching $1.178bn total revenue). However, these customers haven't bought anything in a long time.
Why this happened: These buyers brought in significant value during past periods, but the company didn't follow up with retention campaigns as their activity dropped. They are our most valuable sleeping asset.
How to fix it: Account managers should use the operational dashboard to filter specifically for At-Risk clients. By sorting them by high monetary value and targeting those sitting in extended inactivity groups, we can run personal win-back campaigns to recover this capital.

Pattern 2: The Churn Bottleneck (Strategic Insight)
What I found: The inactivity chart on the operations page shows a heavy pile-up of dormant users. A massive chunk of the base sits in the highest inactivity bracket (>180 days / 400+ days), pushing our average inactivity time for flagged groups up to 379 days, with an At-Risk revenue potential of over $677.8 million. 
Why this happened: The business suffers from a retention gap. Once a customer makes their initial purchase, automated follow-ups are missing, causing users to naturally drift past critical lifecycle thresholds.
How to fix it:
-Immediate fix: Managers should use the color-coded table on the operations page to spot critical rows and offer special incentives to reactivate them.
-Long-term fix: Set up automated engagement sequences early in the lifecycle to prevent users from slipping into the dormant zone.

Pattern 3: Revenue Stability vs. Stagnant Growth (Operational Insight)
What I found: Looking at the quarterly performance on the first page, total revenue stands at a solid baseline of $1.178bn, but the quarterly growth trend fluctuates tightly around stable bounds. Product lines (Laptops, Tablets, Smartphones, Headphones, and Coffee Machines) share the revenue distribution relatively evenly at roughly 20% each, showing uniform performance without a breakout leader.
Why this happened: The business relies heavily on a steady baseline of standard sales, but lacks strong drivers for net-new market expansion or cross-category upselling.
How to fix it: Leadership should introduce product bundling (e.g., combining Laptops with Headphones) or targeted promotional campaigns during historically slower quarters to break the flat-growth ceiling.

Pattern 4: The "At-Risk" Concentration Trap (Operational Insight)
What I found: The RFM segmentation page reveals that while our "Champions" segment drives a healthy 28% revenue share with an average revenue per customer of ~$255,882 (across 979 users), the "At Risk" segment remains our largest numerical risk pool (1,973 customers generating over $612.18m). 
Why this happened: Past marketing successfully acquired and monetized a broad audience, but post-purchase retention strategies failed to secure long-term loyalty for a large portion of buyers.
How to fix it: The director must shift strategic focus toward protecting core assets via VIP loyalty programs for Champions, while simultaneously funding automated reactivation initiatives for the At-Risk portfolio.



DEMO SPEECH TEXT:
Good morning/afternoon. Today, I am excited to present our new Customer Lifecycle & Operations Analytics Suite. I built this tool to bridge the gap between high-level business strategy and daily operations.

First, on the data architecture and engineering side:
Before building the visuals, I designed a star schema in PostgreSQL, separating our core transaction fact tables from dimensional tables like customers and products. To ensure high performance and prevent data duplication, all financial metrics are powered by explicit DAX measures. For instance, instead of using implicit sums that can duplicate rows in a star schema, I utilized SUMX combined with strict status filters ([status] = "Completed") to calculate Total Revenue accurately at a solid baseline of $1.178bn across 13K orders and 4,602 customers. Furthermore, for advanced calculations and percentage distributions, I leveraged context modifiers like REMOVEFILTERS and ALLSELECTED to compute accurate share-of-total ratios without breaking user slicers.

Second, looking at our executive overview (Pages 1 & 2):
Our product lines—Laptops, Tablets, Smartphones, Headphones, and Coffee Machines—are performing very evenly, each capturing around 20% of the market share. However, our quarterly growth trend remains flat, indicating strong baseline sales but a need for targeted bundling or promotions to drive net-new growth.

Third, looking at our customer portfolio (Page 3):
Our RFM segmentation reveals a major hidden risk. Our largest customer group is the 'At-Risk' segment, comprising 1,973 customers. Meanwhile, our healthy 'Champions' generate 28% of total revenue. This means a massive share of company value is locked in accounts that are drifting away due to a lack of follow-up retention campaigns. To secure this environment, I implemented a multi-role Row-Level Security (RLS) architecture. By tying RLS rules directly to our dimension and security tables, we ensure that a regional manager—such as our Warsaw lead—physically sees only their respective branch boundaries and targeted customer risk tiers, completely isolating regional data views.

Finally, how do we fix this operationally? (Page 4):
This is where the Customer Operations Action Center comes in. On the operations page, we track 1,973 At-Risk customers with an average inactivity of 379 days, representing an At-Risk revenue potential of over $677.8 million. 

Instead of guessing who to call, account managers can now open this dashboard, use the color-coded risk table, and instantly spot high-value clients sitting in the critical zone to run targeted win-back campaigns today. In short: the strategic views show leadership where the money is, and the operational view gives managers the exact tools to save it.
